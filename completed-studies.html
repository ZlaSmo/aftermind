Completed Studies

Phase 1 study of intravenous DMT in healthy volunteers

Study overview

The Phase 1 clinical trial, conducted at the Centre for Human Drug Research (CHDR) in Leiden, the Netherlands, evaluated the pharmacokinetics and safety of intravenous DMT given as a 30-second bolus followed by a six-hour continuous infusion. The regimen is sub-psychedelic and covers the hours in which ischemic brain tissue is still at risk.

Key findings

  • No serious adverse events were reported with the bolus-plus-six-hour infusion regimen.
  • Peak plasma exposure was approximately 35 ng/mL, consistent with a sub-psychedelic regimen.
  • Human pharmacokinetics and safety are now established for the regimen that will be used, unchanged, in the Phase 2a acute ischemic stroke study.

Preclinical study of DMT in experimental ischemic stroke

Study overview

The study evaluated DMT in a rat model of transient middle cerebral artery occlusion (tMCAO). Animals underwent a 60-minute occlusion, and DMT treatment started before reperfusion. Lesion volume was measured by MRI at 24 hours (n=10 per group), and recovery of the affected forelimb was followed for 30 days (n=8 per group). A third group received DMT together with BD1063, a selective Sigma-1 receptor antagonist, to test the mechanism.

Key findings

  • DMT reduced mean 24-hour MRI lesion volume to 135.1 ± 19.0 mm³, compared with 204.8 ± 24.5 mm³ in controls, a 34% reduction (p=0.037).
  • Recovery of the stroke-affected forelimb improved from Day 4 through Day 30. At Day 30, DMT-treated animals scored 6.1 versus 1.6 in controls (p=0.008).
  • BD1063 abolished the lesion-volume effect, supporting a Sigma-1 receptor-mediated mechanism.

Preclinical study of DMT and blood–brain barrier protection

Study overview

The study combined the rat tMCAO stroke model with primary blood–brain barrier co-culture experiments. It measured infarct volume and cerebral edema at 24 hours, and blood–brain barrier permeability using leakage of labeled albumin into brain tissue and cerebrospinal fluid.

Key findings

  • DMT reduced infarct volume and associated cerebral edema at 24 hours (P=0.0327).
  • DMT lowered albumin leakage into brain parenchyma (P=0.0093) and cerebrospinal fluid (P=0.0454).
  • DMT preserved tight-junction integrity and reduced inflammatory signaling in the blood–brain barrier model.

In vitro study of DMT in human neurons under hypoxia

Study overview

Human iPSC-derived neurons were exposed to hypoxia and treated with increasing concentrations of DMT. Neuronal viability was measured over time.

Key findings

  • DMT increased survival of human neurons under hypoxia.
  • Protection was dose-dependent: it was seen at higher concentrations (50 µM and above) and not at 1 µM.

Phase 2a study of intravenous DMT in acute ischemic stroke

Study overview

The randomized Phase 2a study will enroll 40 patients with acute ischemic stroke caused by large-vessel occlusion at Semmelweis University in Budapest, Hungary. Patients will receive the six-hour IV DMT regimen established in Phase 1, started around thrombectomy, with 24-hour imaging and clinical follow-up through Day 90. The principal investigator is Dr. Sándor Nardai; Validcare is the CRO.

Endpoints

  • Primary: safety in acute stroke patients receiving standard care.
  • Efficacy signals: imaging, motor and cognitive outcomes.

Preclinical study of DMT in myocardial ischemia–reperfusion injury

Study overview

The study evaluated DMT in a rat model of myocardial infarction. Anesthetized, mechanically ventilated rats underwent occlusion of the left anterior descending (LAD) coronary artery for 45 minutes, followed by 2 hours of reperfusion. Animals were randomized to receive saline or a single intravenous bolus of DMT (10 mg/kg) 15 minutes before occlusion. Infarct size was measured with Evans Blue and TTC staining as a percentage of the area at risk, and ECG was monitored continuously to record ventricular tachycardia (VT) and ventricular fibrillation (VF).

Key findings

  • DMT reduced average infarct size from approximately 23% of the area at risk to under 8% (p<0.05), a relative reduction of about 65%.
  • The incidence of life-threatening arrhythmias, VF and VT, was notably lower in the DMT group during reperfusion.
  • The results indicate that DMT preserves heart muscle and improves electrical stability during reperfusion.

Group sizes are not reported in the study report. These data are unpublished.

Powered preclinical study of DMT in myocardial ischemia

Study overview

A larger, statistically powered study is under way at the University of Debrecen and its Veterinary Faculty to confirm and extend the initial findings. Rats are randomized into control, ischemia-only and ischemia-plus-DMT groups. Ischemia is induced with a titrated isoproterenol protocol, and cardiac structure and function are measured with high-frequency echocardiography (Vevo 3100) and continuous ECG.

Endpoints

  • Ejection fraction, ventricular volumes, wall thickness, stroke volume and diastolic filling.
  • Infarct size (TTC staining) and arrhythmia incidence (VF/VT).
  • Data are reviewed blind, following American Society of Echocardiography guidelines.

Results from this study will support regulatory engagement and IND-enabling work for the myocardial infarction program.

Preclinical lifespan study of DMT in C. elegans

Study overview

The study assessed whether DMT affects longevity in the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans, a standard model in aging research. Synchronized wild-type (N2) worms at the L4 stage were placed on plates containing DMSO control, 2 µM or 5 µM DMT, about 50 worms per plate, with three independent biological replicates per condition. Worms were kept at 20°C in darkness, moved to fresh plates daily and scored for survival blind to treatment. Survival was analyzed with Kaplan–Meier curves and the log-rank test.

Key findings

  • DMT increased median lifespan in a dose-dependent manner: about 11 days in controls, 14.5 days at 2 µM and 16 days at 5 µM.
  • At 5 µM, median lifespan was about 45% longer than in controls, a statistically significant difference (p<0.05).
  • The 2 µM effect approached but did not reach significance, and the two doses did not differ significantly, which may indicate a plateau in effect.

Next steps

  • Healthspan measures such as motility, reproduction and stress resistance.
  • Molecular profiling of aging-related pathways.
  • Validation in other strains and in mammalian models, with dose optimization.

Preliminary study with a modest sample size; survival was the only outcome measured. Relevance to mammals has not been established.
