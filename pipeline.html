About IV DMT for acute ischemic stroke

Our lead program is an intravenous formulation of N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT) being developed as an add-on to standard acute stroke care, including after reperfusion. It is given as a short bolus followed by a six-hour infusion at a sub-psychedelic dose.

DMT activates the Sigma-1 receptor, which helps cells survive oxygen loss, limits inflammation and stabilizes the blood–brain barrier. Through 5-HT2A and BDNF signaling it also promotes neuroplasticity that may support recovery.

The regimen has completed a Phase 1 study at the Centre for Human Drug Research in Leiden, with no serious adverse events. A randomized 40-patient Phase 2a study in acute ischemic stroke is ready to launch at Semmelweis University in Budapest.

IV DMT is an investigational product and has not been approved by the FDA or any other regulatory body.

About DMT for myocardial infarction

We are developing DMT to protect heart muscle during and after a heart attack, when restoring blood flow can itself cause further damage (ischemia–reperfusion injury).

In a rat model of myocardial ischemia–reperfusion, a single intravenous dose of DMT reduced infarct size from about 23% to under 8% of the area at risk and lowered the incidence of ventricular arrhythmias. A larger, statistically powered study is under way at the University of Debrecen.

These preclinical data are unpublished. DMT is an investigational product and has not been approved for any indication.

About our discovery and formulation work

Our in-silico platform designs DMT derivatives that keep the protective receptor profile while reducing 5-HT2A activity. Thirty candidates are ready for synthesis, and one AI-designed molecule has already been made in the lab.

Alongside the IV formulation, we are developing nasal, transdermal, subdermal and oral delivery systems to bring DMT into different clinical settings. One formulation patent application is published and four are in preparation.
